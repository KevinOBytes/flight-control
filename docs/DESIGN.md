# VectorFlight Mathematical and Kinematics Design

This document details the mathematical model, kinematics transformations, and optimization design for the VectorFlight nested thrust-vectoring multirotor control-allocation stack.

## 1. Frame Conventions

We define four distinct coordinate frames:
1. **Body Frame ($B$):** Origin at the vehicle Center of Mass (COM). Right-handed FRD: $+X$ forward, $+Y$ right, $+Z$ down.
2. **Pod Base Frame ($P_{0,j}$):** Origin at the geometric center of Pod $j$. Aligned with the pod's nominal orientation on the airframe.
3. **Pod Gimbal Frame ($P_j$):** Origin at the pod center. Rotated relative to $P_{0,j}$ by the active pod gimbal angles $\alpha_j, \beta_j$.
4. **Rotor Frame ($R_i$):** Origin at the rotor hub. Rotated relative to the pod gimbal frame by the motor tilt angle $\gamma_i$.

## 2. Composed Kinematics

The rotation quaternion mapping from the body frame to the rotor frame $i$ is:
$$R_{B \to R_i} = R_{B \to P_{0,j}} \cdot R_{P_{0,j} \to P_j}(\alpha_j, \beta_j) \cdot R_{P_j \to M_{0,i}} \cdot R_{M_{0,i} \to R_i}(\gamma_i)$$

Where:
- $R_{B \to P_{0,j}}$ is the configured pod mounting orientation.
- $R_{P_{0,j} \to P_j}(\alpha_j, \beta_j) = \text{Rot}(\hat{u}_1, \alpha_j) \cdot \text{Rot}(\hat{u}_2, \beta_j)$, representing rotations about the two orthogonal gimbal axes defined in the pod configuration.
- $R_{P_j \to M_{0,i}}$ is the mounting orientation of the motor frame relative to the pod.
- $R_{M_{0,i} \to R_i}(\gamma_i) = \text{Rot}(\hat{v}, \gamma_i)$ is the rotation about the motor's local tilt axis $\hat{v}$.

### Position Vector in Body Frame
The physical location of the rotor hub in the body frame is:
$$r_i = p_{\text{pod}, j} + R_{B \to P_j}(\alpha_j, \beta_j) \cdot p_{\text{rotor}, i}$$
where:
- $p_{\text{pod}, j}$ is the pod's location relative to the COM.
- $p_{\text{rotor}, i}$ is the rotor's local offset within the pod structure.

## 3. Forward Wrench Formulation

A rotor spinning with thrust magnitude $T_i \ge 0$ creates:
1. **Thrust force:**
   $$F_i = T_i \cdot n_i$$
   where $n_i$ is the unit direction vector in body frame:
   $$n_i = R_{B \to R_i} \cdot \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix}$$
   (Since $+Z$ is down, a positive upward lift thrust acts along the $-Z$ rotor direction).

2. **Reaction torque:**
   $$M_{\text{reaction}, i} = s_i \cdot k_t \cdot T_i \cdot n_i$$
   where $s_i \in \{-1, +1\}$ is the spin direction sign (CW: +1, CCW: -1) and $k_t$ is the torque-to-thrust coefficient.

3. **Composed Moment:**
   The total moment generated by rotor $i$ about the COM is:
   $$M_i = (r_i - r_{\text{COM}}) \times F_i + M_{\text{reaction}, i}$$

The total wrench $W = [F_x, F_y, F_z, M_x, My, M_z]^T$ is the sum of forces and moments over all 16 rotors:
$$W = \sum_{i=1}^{16} \begin{bmatrix} F_i \\ M_i \end{bmatrix}$$

## 4. Hierarchical Optimization Strategy

Solving a 40-variable nonlinear optimizer in a hard real-time loop is computationally expensive and introduces non-deterministic execution times. We partition the problem into a hierarchy:

```text
               Desired 6-DOF Wrench
                        │
                        ▼
           ┌─────────────────────────┐
           │   Pod Tilt Planner      │   ~ 20 Hz
           │ (Gross vector direction)│
           └────────────┬────────────┘
                        │
                        ▼
           ┌─────────────────────────┐
           │   Motor Tilt Planner    │   ~ 50 Hz
           │  (Vector refinement)    │
           └────────────┬────────────┘
                        │
                        ▼
           ┌─────────────────────────┐
           │   Thrust QP Allocator   │   ~ 200 Hz
           │ (Fast moment/force correction)
           └─────────────────────────┘
```

### 1. Fast Thrust Allocation QP
At 200 Hz, with pod angles ($\alpha_j, \beta_j$) and motor tilt angles ($\gamma_i$) fixed, the forward wrench is linear with respect to the thrust vector $f = [T_1, \dots, T_{16}]^T$:
$$W = B \cdot f$$
where $B \in \mathbb{R}^{6 \times 16}$ is the linear thrust-effectiveness matrix. We formulate the QP:
$$\min_{\Delta f} \frac{1}{2} \Delta f^T P \Delta f + q^T \Delta f$$
subject to:
$$f_{\min} \le f_k + \Delta f \le f_{\max}$$
$$\Delta f_{\min} \le \Delta f \le \Delta f_{\max}$$

The objective matrix $P$ and vector $q$ incorporate:
- Tracking error penalization: $W_e \cdot \|B \cdot (f_k + \Delta f) - W_{\text{des}}\|^2$
- Thrust deviation minimization: $\lambda_{\text{smooth}} \cdot \|\Delta f\|^2$
- Actuator centering: $\lambda_{\text{center}} \cdot \|f_k + \Delta f - f_{\text{nominal}}\|^2$

### 2. Tilt Planners
At slower rates, the local Jacobian $J = \frac{\partial W}{\partial U}$ is used to solve for small increments in motor and pod tilt angles to optimize control margins, centering variables, and thrust efficiency.

## 5. Fault Incorporation

Faults are not handled by custom mixing overrides. Instead, a fault directly modifies the QP constraint bounds:
- **Failed Motor:** $T_{i, \min} = T_{i, \max} = 0$.
- **Jammed Joint:** Gimbal limits $\gamma_{i, \min} = \gamma_{i, \max} = \gamma_{\text{jammed}}$.
- **Degraded Actuator:** Limits are scaled down based on health status reports.
This guarantees the solver naturally accounts for degradation without creating singular execution branches.
